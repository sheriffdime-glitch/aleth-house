Policies

Privacy, in plain sight.

Effective October 10, 2026 · Version 2.0

Truth is our whole business model. It covers your data too. We collect what an order needs, we never sell anything about you, and we do not follow you around the internet.

This policy is written to be read, not survived. It states what we collect, why we collect it, who ever touches it, and how long we keep it — in that order, with no reservation hidden in a definition. Where a promise could be read two ways, we have written the narrower one. Where a practice could be excused as an industry habit, we have named the habit and refused it. Your Personal Information is not our product and never will be. It is the residue of a transaction you chose to make with us, and it is owed exactly the care that transaction is owed: no more collection than an Order requires, no wider use than this policy states, and no less protection than we would demand for our own formulas.

Article 1Definitions

1.1"Personal Information" means information that identifies, relates to, or could reasonably be linked with you. It includes the plain facts of an Order — a name, a shipping or billing address, an email address — and the technical residue of a visit, such as an IP address sitting alongside the pages a browser viewed. What it does not include is information genuinely stripped of identity and beyond reasonable reconnection to you; nothing here treats truly anonymous data as your Personal Information, and nothing here permits anyone to attempt to re-identify such data.

1.2"Process," "Processing" means collecting, storing, using, sharing, or deleting Personal Information. Each verb is a distinct act, and every act this policy describes is one of them. Processing is justified only by a purpose named in Article 3, only for as long as Article 10 permits, and only through the parties Article 5 allows. When the purpose ends and no law holds the record open, Processing ends with it.

1.3"Service Provider" means a company that processes data strictly on our behalf, under contract, for the purposes below. The contract is the whole point of the definition. A Service Provider acts with our authority and none of its own: it may not use what it receives for its own purposes, may not fold it into products of its own, may not sell it or share it for advertising, and its handling of your data is bounded by this policy exactly as if we were doing the work ourselves. A company that would need your data for its own account is not a Service Provider under this definition — and does not receive it.

1.4"Customer," "you" means the person this policy protects: whoever places an Order, considers one, writes to us, or simply reads the Site. A visitor who never buys anything is covered by every word here exactly as a Customer is. Where an Order is placed by one person for delivery to another — a gift, a shipment to a worksite — this policy governs the information given for both, and both are owed the same restraint.

1.5"Site" means alethchemical.com and its pages; "Order" means a request to purchase Products from ALETH; "Products" means the goods ALETH sells. These terms carry their ordinary commercial meaning and nothing wider.

1.6"Sale" and "Share" carry the meanings California law gives those words, and appear in Article 9 because California defines them with unusual precision. Stated once here so the point is impossible to miss: ALETH does neither.

Article 2What We Collect

2.1Information you give us: name, shipping and billing address, email address, phone number (optional), and order details. Each item has exactly one job. The name and the address move a package through the world; the email address carries the confirmations a purchase generates; the phone number is marked optional because it is — an Order does not depend on it, and you may leave it blank without consequence. "Order details" means the record of the transaction itself: what you bought, in what quantity, on what date, at what price, bound for which address. Where an Order names a recipient other than yourself, we collect that recipient's name and delivery address for delivery and for nothing else. The list in this clause is not illustrative. It is the complete list.

2.2Payment information: card payments are processed by our payment processor. We receive confirmation and limited payment metadata (last four digits, card type) — we never see or store your full card number. The full card number is not a secret we happen to keep badly; it is a secret we never hold at all. It does not pass through our logs, our backups, or our support tooling. If a refund is owed, it returns along the path it came — through the processor. If a charge is disputed, we work from the confirmation and the metadata, which is all we have and all we want.

2.3Information collected automatically: device and browser type, IP address, pages viewed, and cart contents. Your cart lives in your browser's own storage and does not touch our servers until you check out. Until that moment, what you place in a cart is visible to you and to your browser and to no one here; clear your browser storage and the cart empties, which is exactly the design. Automatic collection serves three ends and no others: keeping the Site secure, detecting fraud, and understanding in aggregate how the Site performs. Your IP address is processed as a matter of network mechanics and security, not as a means of building a profile of you.

2.4Communications: messages you send us, and your marketing preferences. What you write to support — including anything you attach — is processed to answer you and to keep a record of the answer, so that if the same question returns, the history is intact. Your marketing preferences are themselves a small record we keep deliberately: what you said yes to, and what you said no to. An opt-out is remembered so that it can be honored; silence is never read as consent.

2.5The categories above are the complete inventory of Personal Information ALETH collects. We do not collect what an Order does not need. There is no hidden fifth category, no enrichment append, no purchased dossier waiting behind the checkout page.

Article 3Why We Use It

3.1To fulfill and deliver Orders, process payments, and handle returns or claims under our policies. This is the core of the transaction you asked for: assembling what you bought, moving it to where you sent it, settling what you paid, and resolving what goes wrong when something arrives other than as it should. It includes the order-related communications a purchase inherently requires, including the shipment-timing notices contemplated by the FTC Mail, Internet, or Telephone Order Merchandise Rule, 16 CFR Part 435.

3.2To answer your questions and provide customer care. Correspondence is read by people, answered by people, and kept long enough to be useful if the matter returns. Customer care is a use of your data only in the sense that answering a letter is a use of its contents.

3.3To send marketing email only if you asked for it — and to send transactional messages about your Orders (confirmations, shipping, recalls or safety notices). Transactional messages include product-safety information that accompanies Products in the ordinary course: recall notices, handling warnings, and the California Proposition 65 warnings that apply to particular Products where they apply. These follow the transaction, not the newsletter.

3.4To detect fraud, secure the Site, and comply with legal obligations (tax, product safety, regulatory recordkeeping). Some processing is ours to choose; some is the law's to demand. Where a record must exist because a tax authority, a product-safety regime, or a regulator requires it, that record exists for that reason, and its life is governed by Article 10.

3.5To understand, in aggregate, how the Site is used — so we can make it better. Aggregate is the operative word. Analytics tells us which pages confuse visitors and where the Site fails them; it is a view of patterns, not of people. We cannot improve what we cannot observe, and we have drawn the line at observing less than the pattern requires.

Every use of your Personal Information traces to one of these five purposes. There is no sixth purpose, and no purpose waiting to be discovered later without this page changing first.

Article 4What We Never Do

4.1The floor. We do not sell your Personal Information. Not for money, not for data, not in trade. Ever. A promise that covers only money is not a promise; ours covers every device the industry has invented for moving a person's life sideways while calling it something else — list rental, brokered audience segments, "data enrichment" partnerships, hashed identifiers matched against someone else's advertising platform, revenue shared with anyone who would use your name for their own marketing. None of it. In no form. For no price. Not as a product, not as a line item, not as a favor to a partner, and not as a term buried in an agreement we hope you do not read. If a transfer of your Personal Information would be a sale under any honest reading, it does not happen here.

4.2We do not share it for cross-context behavioral advertising. We do not run advertising pixels or retargeting cookies. ALETH does not follow you around the internet. There is no third-party advertising tag on our pages, no conversion pixel feeding someone else's model, no audience syndicated to a platform so that our Products can chase you after you leave. Our view of you begins and ends at our own pages: what you do here, we can see in aggregate; what you do elsewhere, we neither observe, buy, infer, nor want. The advertising industry's entire apparatus of surveillance runs on the data companies hand it. We hand it nothing.

4.3We do not rent, trade, or broker your data to data aggregators. Aggregators exist to assemble people into commodities; nothing from ALETH enters that assembly. Your name does not become a row in someone else's warehouse, your address does not confirm someone else's record of you, and your purchase does not sharpen a profile held by a party you have never heard of.

4.4If any of this ever changes, it will never happen silently — you would see it here first, with a clear way to say no. This page changes before any practice does, never after; the change appears with a new effective date under Article 13, it applies prospectively and never retroactively, and a refusal path stands ready before the new practice is switched on. A promise broken quietly is not a change of policy. It is a breach of one, and we have written this clause so that it cannot happen by anyone's convenience, including our own.

Article 5Who We Share With

5.1Service Providers, under contract, only as needed: payment processor; shipping carriers; email delivery provider; privacy-focused website analytics; and our hosting and security vendors. "Only as needed" is a real limit, not a courtesy phrase. Each Service Provider receives the minimum that its task requires and nothing adjacent to it: the carrier needs a name and an address to deliver a package and receives no payment data, because the payment is not its business; the email provider needs an address to deliver a message and receives nothing for its own account; the analytics provider sees usage patterns and is bound to privacy-focused measurement with no advertising use. Every one of these relationships is governed by the definition in 1.3, and every one of them ends where our instruction ends. Shipping carriers also receive the shipping and dangerous-goods information that applicable law requires of a shipment, including where 49 CFR 173.150 applies. None of these recipients may sell what they receive from us; the prohibition in Article 4 does not travel loosely — it binds anyone who touches your data on our behalf.

5.2Legal and regulatory authorities, when we are legally compelled, or when necessary to protect rights, safety, and the integrity of our Products (including product-safety obligations). Compulsion means compulsion: a valid subpoena, court order, or statute, honored to its exact scope and not a sentence beyond it. Where we are legally permitted to tell you that a demand has been made for your information, the presumption favors telling you. Where a disclosure is not compelled but necessary — to protect a person's safety, to defend a legal right, or to meet a product-safety obligation attached to Products we have sold — it is made because the alternative is worse, and it is made to the party who genuinely needs it. What we never do is volunteer your information to anyone in the hope of currying favor with an authority.

5.3A successor entity, in the event of a business reorganization — under the same promises made here. The promises travel with the data. If ALETH is reorganized, merged, or its assets are transferred, your Personal Information moves only to a successor bound by these same commitments, and the record of you does not arrive anywhere lighter on protection than it left. A successor that would not keep these promises does not inherit the data as though it were ordinary property — Article 4.4 governs before anything moves.

Article 6Cookies & Similar Technologies

6.1We use strictly necessary cookies and browser storage (cart, security, preferences) and limited first-party analytics to understand aggregate Site usage. Each category earns its place by being necessary or by being ours alone: the cart lives in your browser's storage under 2.3; security cookies keep a session intact and help detect fraud; preference storage remembers choices you made on the Site so you do not have to make them twice; first-party analytics observes the Site's own pages in aggregate, so that we can see where the Site fails you. A cookie that exists to serve someone else's advertising does not belong to any of these categories, and does not exist on our pages.

6.2We do not use advertising cookies, retargeting pixels, or third-party ad tracking. We do not buy audiences and we do not sell ours. There is no tag on this Site whose purpose is to identify you to another company; if one ever appeared, it would contradict Article 4, and this clause would be rewritten before the tag shipped, not after.

6.3Global Privacy Control (GPC): we honor the GPC signal. If your browser sends it, we treat it as a request to opt out of any sale or sharing of Personal Information — which, per Article 4, we do not do anyway. The signal is honored as sent: you do not have to prove it, repeat it, or pair it with any other step, and no setting of ours is permitted to contradict a browser that has spoken. Where the signal arrives, the answer it carries is treated as the answer, on this visit and on those that follow.

Article 7Marketing Communications

7.1Email marketing is sent only with your consent (for example, when you join our list). Every marketing email carries a working unsubscribe link; opt-outs are honored within 10 business days. Consent means a deliberate act: joining the list is one; buying a Product is not one, and neither is silence, an unchecked box doing some other job, or the mere fact that an address reached us through an Order. The unsubscribe link is a promise, not decoration — it works in the message you actually received, it works once, and the request it carries needs no explanation, no login, and no negotiation. Ten business days is the outer limit of the honor period, not its target, and an opt-out is never met with a counter-offer.

7.2Transactional messages (order, shipping, safety) are not marketing and are not affected by marketing opt-outs. They belong to the transaction, not to the newsletter: order confirmations, payment receipts, shipping and delivery notices, returns correspondence, recall notices, handling warnings, and California Proposition 65 warnings where they apply to a Product you bought. This separation is deliberate. Withdrawing from marketing must never cost you the safety information a purchase generates — the law obliges some of these messages, and the rest follow from the fact that something you bought is moving through the world under our name.

7.3SMS/text messaging is not offered at this time. If it ever is, it will require separate, unbundled written consent with its own program terms. "Unbundled" means it cannot ride on an Order, on an email signup, on a terms checkbox that does something else, or on silence — it would stand alone, in writing, revocable on its own, and this clause would be replaced before a single message shipped.

Article 8Your Rights

8.1You may ask us to: access the Personal Information we hold about you; correct it; delete it; provide a copy (portability); or opt out of marketing — at any time. Access means an account of what exists; correction means fixing what is wrong and, where a correction propagates, where it was sent; deletion means removal of what we are not legally required to keep; portability means a copy in a usable form, so that your information is yours to carry and not ours to hold hostage; the marketing opt-out stands exactly as Article 7 defines it. "At any time" is literal — these rights do not expire with the Order, the relationship, or the calendar.

8.2How: email support@alethchemical.com with "Privacy Request" in the subject. We respond within 45 days, in plain language, free of charge. One channel, plainly labeled, because a right that requires a scavenger hunt is not a right. A human reads the request, and where we must confirm that the request is genuinely yours before disclosing or deleting anything, we verify — not to obstruct, but because handing your data to an impostor would be the cruelest possible way to honor you. The answer comes in language you can act on, without footnotes designed to exhaust you. Free of charge, without exception: a right you must purchase is a privilege, and we do not sell those.

8.3We do not discriminate against anyone for exercising these rights. We will not deny you Products, charge you a different price, provide a different level of service or quality, or treat a request as a reason to look at you differently. The price of a Product is the price of the Product; it does not move because you asked what we know about you.

Article 9State Notices (United States)

9.1California: we do not "sell" or "share" Personal Information as those terms are defined by California law. California residents have rights to know, delete, correct, and opt out of sale/sharing, and to limit use of sensitive Personal Information. Because Article 4 forbids the conduct those verbs describe, the opt-out of sale and sharing has nothing to operate on — but the right stands, it is honored, and it is honored through the ordinary process in Article 8, not through a special door reserved for one state. The right to limit the use of sensitive Personal Information is honored the same way, and we note that ALETH does not build the kind of profile that would make that limit necessary in the first place.

9.2Residents of other states with comprehensive privacy laws (including Colorado, Connecticut, Virginia, Texas, Oregon, and others) have similar rights — access, correction, deletion, portability, and opt-out of targeted advertising or sale. We honor all of them uniformly, wherever you live. Uniform means uniform: a Customer in Oregon is not offered less than a Customer in California because a legislature happened to write the rule later, and a Customer in a state that has not yet legislated is not offered less than either. The list of states will grow as laws are enacted; our answer will not change when it does. Use the process in Article 8 — the same channel, the same 45 days, the same plain language, the same price, which is none.

9.3Authorized agents may submit requests on your behalf with verifiable authorization. An agent who writes on your behalf must show that they act for you — verifiably, not plausibly — and we verify before anything is disclosed or deleted, for the same reason we verify a direct request: the request is honored exactly when its authority is real, and never before. Your own standing rights are not diminished by using an agent; the agent exercises them, they remain yours.

Article 10Retention & Security

10.1We keep Personal Information only as long as needed for the purposes here and for legal obligations — transaction and tax records are generally retained up to 7 years; marketing data until you opt out. "Generally" is honest: tax and commercial law sets floors that move, and a record whose retention period a statute commands is kept exactly as long as the statute commands, no longer. When a purpose ends and no law holds the record open, the data is deleted or stripped of identity. Even an opt-out leaves a small trace on purpose — the record of your refusal is kept so that the refusal can be honored; it is not a back door to contact you again. Marketing data does not outlive your opt-out except as that record of the opt-out itself.

10.2We use reasonable technical and organizational measures to protect your data. No system is perfectly secure, and we will never pretend otherwise — but we treat your data the way we treat our formulas: locked down. Access to Personal Information is granted only to those whose work requires it, and only for as long as it does; protections are sized to the nature of the information and to the harm its exposure would cause. Anyone who promises you certainty in security is selling something that does not exist. What we promise is effort, restraint, and honesty about the residual risk — which is the only thing a serious company can promise and keep.

Article 11Breach Notification

11.1If a security incident affects your Personal Information, we will notify you and regulators as required by applicable law — including, for North Carolina residents, without unreasonable delay under N.C.G.S. § 75-65. An incident is met the way a contamination is met in a lab: contained first, scoped second, disclosed third and on purpose. We establish what was accessed and whose information it was before we write to anyone, because a notice that guesses is worse than silence — and then the notice goes out within whatever window the law sets, and "without unreasonable delay" is read as a limit on us, not as room to deliberate. Where the law requires notice to a regulator, that notice is made; where it requires notice to you, you hear from us directly, in plain language, with what happened, what it means for you, and what we are doing about it.

11.2A security incident is never handled as a public-relations problem. The obligations in this Article stand alongside Article 10 — the same honesty that admits no system is perfectly secure commits us to saying so out loud when security fails. No provision of this policy, and no arrangement with any Service Provider, is permitted to delay or dilute a notification that applicable law requires.

Article 12Children

12.1The Site is not directed to children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect their Personal Information. If we learn that we have, we delete it. This is a standard we hold without exception, and deletion on discovery is the whole remedy — not review, not restriction, not a shrunk-down account.

12.2If you are a parent or guardian and believe a child under 13 has given us Personal Information — through an Order, a message, or any other path — write to support@alethchemical.com and we will delete it. We do not market to children, we do not build audiences of them, and there is nothing on this Site designed to hold their attention or their data. A brand that must be trusted with chemistry must be trusted with far more than chemistry.

Article 13Changes & Contact

13.1Material changes to this policy will be posted here with a new effective date, and where appropriate communicated by email or Site notice. Changes apply prospectively. A change is material when it touches what we collect, why we collect it, who ever touches it, how long we keep it, or anything in Article 4 — in other words, when it touches something this policy exists to tell you. The posting is not buried and the date is not fudged: the version at the top of this page changes, the old version's meaning is never quietly rewritten, and the list of recipients in Article 5 does not widen by a single name before this page says so first. Your past is not altered by a future edit of this page; obligations that arose under the policy as it stood are honored under the policy as it stood.

13.2Privacy questions and requests: support@alethchemical.com. A human answers. Not a ticket that expires, not a bot that loops, and not a form engineered to make you give up — a person, who reads what you wrote and writes back. This Article is the door to every other one; the address never changes without this page changing first.
