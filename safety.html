Product Safety & Handling.

Effective October 10, 2026 · Version 2.0

Professional chemistry rewards respect. Five minutes of care here is worth more than any trick — and it keeps the ritual safe for you, your surfaces, your family, and your pets.

Article 1Read Before You Pour

1.1The label and the SDS govern. Read the label and the Safety Data Sheet (SDS) before every use. Not once at purchase, not from memory, not from a habit formed with some other bottle on some other shelf — before every use. The label states what the Product is built to do: which surfaces, which dilution, which dwell time, which method. The SDS states what the chemistry is, what it can do to the person handling it, and what to do when contact happens. Between the two documents sits everything ALETH knows about the safe use of the Product in your hands, and nothing on this page overrides either of them.

1.2Where both documents live. Both are published for every Product, free and without registration, at alethchemical.com/sds.html. They are there before the sale, at the moment of the sale, and long after it. Print the SDS if you work away from a screen; keep it with the Product. If you are holding a container whose SDS you cannot read, the safe course is simple: do not use that Product until you can. A delay costs minutes; an uninformed pour can cost far more.

1.3Use only as the label directs. Use each Product only on the surfaces named on its label, at the stated dilution and dwell time. These three limits — surface, dilution, dwell — are not suggestions and not conservative estimates. They are the conditions under which the Product was formulated, tested, and warranted. A Product used outside them has left the chemistry ALETH stands behind. If the surface you want is not named on the label, that silence is an answer: ask us first, at support@alethchemical.com.

1.4Dilution is part of the chemistry. A stronger mix is not a stronger result, and a gentler mix is not a gentler one — both are a different product than the one the label describes. Over-concentration can overwhelm a surface and leave residue; under-dilution wastes material and teaches the wrong habit. Measure, do not estimate.

1.5The word "every" in 1.1 is deliberate. Labels are revised, surfaces are refinished, seasons change what a garage or a driveway holds, and the second, tenth, and hundredth use is precisely where familiarity stops being an advantage. The Product has not changed since you last read its label. Your conditions may have. Rereading costs less than a minute.

1.6This page states principle; the documents state facts. Where this page and the label or the SDS speak to the same subject, the label and the SDS govern. They were written for the specific Product in your hand. This page was written for all of them.

Article 2Personal Protection

2.1Gloves and eye protection, always, with concentrates. Always wear gloves and eye protection when handling concentrates. Concentrates are the densest form in which the chemistry reaches you — the moment before dilution, when the margin is smallest and any contact is direct. Handling means opening, pouring, decanting, adjusting a dilution, and wiping up whatever escapes the container. Protection is not ceremonial and it is not measured by experience: a veteran and a first-time customer stand equally unprotected without it. The cost of putting gloves on is counted in seconds. A splash of unprotected chemistry is counted some other way entirely.

2.2Ventilation. Work with ventilation. Outdoors or in an open, ventilated space whenever possible. Open a door, open the garage, work upwind of your own work. A closed space holds whatever the work releases — and it holds it at exactly the height you are breathing at. If the space cannot be ventilated, that is a reason to move the work, not to shorten the protection.

2.3Never eat, drink, or smoke while handling. Never eat, drink, or smoke while handling chemical products. Food handled in the same breath as chemistry carries whatever the air carries. The rule is absolute for the duration of the work — not paused, not minimized, not narrowed to a quick bite between pours.

2.4Wash hands after use. Wash hands after use — after every use, whether or not gloves were worn, whether or not anything spilled, and before touching food, a face, or anything else bound for either. Gloves are the barrier during the work. Washing is the barrier between the work and everything that comes after it.

2.5The whole ritual, not the first pour. Protection covers the entire job, from the first opening to the last closing. The moment that catches people is rarely the dramatic one; it is the unscrewing of a cap, the top-up of a dilution, the carrying of an open container across a floor. Wear the protection for those moments too — they are most of the job.

Article 3Testing & Technique

3.1Test every Product on a small, hidden area first. Test every Product on a small, hidden area first — with any chemical, ours included. Surfaces vary, and honesty starts with a test patch. No formulator, ours or anyone else's, can see every finish, every previous treatment, every summer of sun and every winter of salt from behind a screen. The test patch is where the surface answers for itself.

3.2What a real test patch is. Small, hidden, and honest: the same surface, the same conditions, the same dilution, the same dwell time, and the same method you intend for the whole job — only out of sight. Under an edge, inside a wheel barrel, behind a trim piece, in a corner no one will look at twice. A test at full strength on a scrap piece is not a test of what you are about to do, and a test performed differently than the job is not a test of the job.

3.3Test again when the conditions change. A test patch answers for that surface, on that day. A refinished panel, a different wheel, a repainted trim piece, a surface that has aged, cured, or been treated since your last visit — each is a new question, and each deserves its own patch. The test patch is not a one-time gate at the start of ownership; it is the first step of every job.

3.4Follow the method on the label — dwell time is chemistry. Follow the method on the label. Dwell time is chemistry: leaving a product on far longer than directed does not make it better, and can harm the surface. A dwell time is the window in which the reaction was designed to happen. Past that window, the work is no longer the work the label describes. If the result is not there at the stated time, the answer is not a longer wait — write to support@alethchemical.com and ask. We would rather answer the question than read about the finish it cost.

3.5Technique is part of the formula. The method on the label is not padding wrapped around the chemistry. It is the third ingredient. The Product, the surface, and the method together produce the result; remove any one of them and you are running an experiment on something you own.

Article 4Storage & Household Safety

4.1Cool, dry, upright, tightly closed. Store Products cool, dry, upright, and tightly closed — away from direct sun, freezing, and heat sources. Heat builds pressure inside a sealed container and accelerates the degradation of a formulation; freezing can separate a formulation and stress the container that holds it; direct sun delivers heat and chemistry at once. Upright keeps the closure where the design put it, and tightly closed is what makes a container a container rather than an open vessel in a room where people live.

4.2Keep all Products away from children and pets. Keep all Products away from children and pets — in storage, in use, and in the minutes between. A container set down on a garage floor sits at exactly the height of a child's curiosity and a dog's nose. The safety closure on a cap is a last line, not a first one; the first line is a location that is out of reach and out of mind.

4.3Never transfer Products into food or drink containers. Never transfer Products into food or drink containers. A Product poured into a soda bottle loses its label, its name, its dilution, its first-aid instructions, its SDS link, and every warning ALETH printed for it. Chemistry in an unmarked container is a hazard without instructions — and to everyone in the house it is indistinguishable from a drink. Keep every Product in its original container, with its label intact and legible. The label is part of the Product; separate them and you no longer hold what you bought.

4.4Never mix Products. Never mix Products — with one another, or with any other chemical. Mixtures can release dangerous gases or destroy the chemistry you paid for. Mixing is the one shortcut in this craft that turns two products used exactly as directed into something no label describes and no SDS covers. ALETH formulates each Product to do a defined job alone; the bottle is not a suggestion, it is the boundary of what was tested and what is known. Household cleaners, solvents, acids, bleach, and anything else already under your sink are not exceptions to this rule — they are the reason for it. If two chemicals have already been combined, the result is unknown: do not use the mixture, and if anyone has been exposed to it, go to Article 5 at once.

4.5Storage is part of the Product. The chemistry in a container continues to be chemistry while it waits for you. The conditions in 4.1 are what keep it the Product you paid for until the day you open it. A formulation that has frozen, baked, stood open, or been split into other vessels has changed — and a Product that has changed is a Product whose label no longer describes it.

Article 5Emergencies

5.1Follow the label and the SDS first — immediately. If a Product is swallowed, inhaled excessively, or gets in eyes or on skin: follow the first-aid instructions on the label and SDS immediately. Immediately means immediately — not after a search, not after a call to a friend, not after the panel you were working on is finished. The first-aid instructions on the label and in the SDS were written for this exact moment and for the exact Product in your hand. This page does not repeat them and does not replace them; it points to them, and it points to them first.

5.2Medical emergencies: 911. Poisoning or exposure questions: Poison Control. For medical emergencies, call 911. For poisoning or exposure questions (U.S.), call Poison Control at 1-800-222-1222 (free, confidential, 24/7). If you can reach it safely, bring the container, the label, or the SDS to the call — the Product name and the SDS are what turn a general question into a specific answer. Do not wait for symptoms before deciding whether a call is warranted. Poison Control exists precisely so that asking costs nothing.

5.3Transportation or shipping incidents. For a transportation or shipping incident, contact emergency services and the carrier directly. The carrier is responsible for the movement of the shipment and knows what it is carrying; emergency services know what an incident requires. Report what you see — the container, the markings, the smell, the spill — and let the people trained for the route take the next step.

5.4After the emergency is handled. When the immediate matter is resolved, tell us: support@alethchemical.com. We want to know what happened, with which Product, under which conditions — not to argue and not to assign blame, but because a report is how a formulation, a label, or a page like this one gets better. The only useless report is the one nobody writes.

Article 6California Proposition 65

6.1What the law requires. California law (Proposition 65) requires warnings when products contain certain listed chemicals above defined levels. Where a warning is required for an ALETH Product, it appears on the product page before purchase and on the label — never buried in a footer. Before you buy and after you buy: the warning travels with the Product, in both places, at both moments. A warning you have to hunt for is not a warning. It is a hope that you would not find it, and ALETH does not publish those.

6.2What a warning is, and what it is not. A Proposition 65 warning is a right-to-know notice. It is not a statement that a Product is unsafe when used as directed, and it is not an accusation. It is California's answer to the questions of who should know what, and when they should know it. If you see one, you have been told what the law says you should be told, in the place the law says you should be told it.

6.3The full list. For the full list of listed chemicals and further information, visit www.P65Warnings.ca.gov. The list changes over time; the State of California maintains it, and we point to the source rather than paraphrase a document that is not ours to rewrite.

6.4If something does not match. If you see an ALETH Product carrying a warning on one surface and not another, or a warning you cannot reconcile with the SDS in front of you, write to support@alethchemical.com and we will reconcile it with you. A disclosure that is inconsistent is a disclosure that is not doing its job.

Article 7Purchase & Shipping Restrictions

7.1Restrictions are shown before you pay. Where federal, state, or local law restricts the sale or transport of particular chemistries (including jurisdictional VOC rules or ground-only transport requirements), those restrictions are shown before you pay — at checkout or on the product page. We never surprise you after the fact. A restriction discovered after payment is not a restriction; it is a problem handed to the wrong person at the wrong time. Pre-sale disclosure is also where the law of the sale expects honesty to live — see UCC Article 2 (N.C.G.S. Ch. 25, Art. 2) and N.C.G.S. Ch. 75.

7.2What the restrictions are about. They are about the chemistry and the route, not about the customer. Some formulations are limited by the air quality rules of the jurisdiction they are shipping into. Some may not travel by air at all and move only by ground, consistent with the limited-quantity provisions of 49 CFR 173.150. A restriction is information about where a Product may go and how it may get there — nothing more, and nothing less.

7.3Details and remedies. Details: see our Shipping Policy, Article 4. Where an order cannot proceed or cannot ship as presented, our obligations on the sale are governed by FTC 16 CFR Part 435 and our Returns & Refund Policy. The restriction belongs on the screen before the payment, and the remedy belongs in a policy written before the dispute.

Article 8Contact

8.1Safety questions before you use a Product: support@alethchemical.com. A human answers — and a safety question jumps the line. Not a form letter, not a queue behind invoices and shipping tickets: a safety question is the one message that moves to the front. Write before you pour if you are unsure. There is no question about a Product that is too small to send and too large to guess at.

8.2What to tell us. The Product name exactly as it appears on the label, the surface you intend to use it on, the method and dilution you plan to follow, and the question you actually have. A photograph of the label answers half of what we would otherwise have to ask. The faster we know what is in your hands, the faster the answer arrives.

8.3Where the lines are drawn. Order and delivery questions belong to our Shipping Policy, Article 5 and our Returns & Refund Policy. Safety questions come here. Emergencies go to Article 5 — to 911 and Poison Control at 1-800-222-1222 — and never to an email inbox.
