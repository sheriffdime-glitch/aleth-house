Precision is for everyone.

Effective October 10, 2026 · Version 2.0

Precision is for everyone. Accessibility is not a courtesy extended after the work is finished; it is a property of the work itself, decided at the same table as formulation, price, and safety. This statement is honest about what we have built, what we are still fixing, and how to reach a human when something gets in your way. It is written to be read — in full, in text, by anyone, on any device, with any tool.

Article 1Our Commitment

1.1ALETH Chemical Co. is committed to making alethchemical.com accessible to all visitors, including people with disabilities. The commitment covers the whole of what we publish on the Site — every page, every document, every form, every policy — and it does not vary by product, by page, or by the size of the order behind the visit.

1.2We aim to conform to the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.1, Level AA, published by the World Wide Web Consortium (W3C) — the same benchmark the U.S. government has adopted as the definition of accessible web content. The word "aim" is chosen with care. We do not claim third-party certification, and we do not state full conformance as an accomplished fact. We claim a standard, a practice, and a standing obligation to close whatever distance remains between the two.

1.3This commitment binds ALETH itself. It is not discharged, diluted, or delegated by the fact that some part of the Site runs on third-party software, or that some page a Customer must pass through is operated by someone else. Where the machinery is ours, the responsibility is ours in full. Where it is not, Article 3 says so plainly, and Article 4 still answers.

1.4What we mean by accessibility is not a statement about our intentions. It is a statement about what a visitor can actually do: reach the page, read the page, navigate the page, complete the form, purchase the Product, and leave with the information they came for — using whatever tools they use to see, hear, and move through the web. A promise a screen reader cannot cash is not a promise.

1.5The standard we hold is the one this Company was founded on. Truth, in formulation, includes the truth of whether a person can use what we built. We would rather say what is imperfect in precise language than say what is comfortable in vague language.

Article 2What We Do

2.1We design and build to WCAG 2.1 AA. In practice that is not one activity but six, and each is a requirement of the build rather than a refinement applied after it:

  • Keyboard navigation
  • Text alternatives for images
  • Sufficient color contrast
  • Labeled form fields
  • Clear error identification
  • Readable structure

Each of those six is named and carried in the sub-clauses below, because a list that is not examined is a list that is not kept.

2.2 Keyboard navigation. Every action the Site offers with a pointer must be reachable and operable with a keyboard alone: navigating between pages, opening menus, selecting options, filling and submitting forms, and returning to where the visitor began. A control that only a mouse can reach is, for some visitors, a control that does not exist.

2.3 Text alternatives. Meaningful images — container labels, Product photography, diagrams, charts — carry text that conveys the same meaning to someone who cannot see the image. Images that carry no information, the purely decorative, are marked so that assistive technology passes over them instead of announcing them as noise. When an image carries Product safety information, the text alternative is treated as part of the safety of the Product, not as an afterthought to the page.

2.4 Sufficient color contrast. Text is set against its background at contrast sufficient to be read by visitors with low vision, on tired screens, and in poor light. Information is never carried by color alone: where color signals meaning — an error, a selection, a warning — the same meaning is carried in text or shape, so that it survives both color blindness and a grayscale printout.

2.5 Labeled form fields and clear error identification. Every field on the Site carries a label that assistive technology can read, so that a visitor entering their shipping address knows which line is the street and which is the state. When a submission fails, the error is identified clearly: it names the field, it says in words what is wrong, and it is announced to screen readers rather than only painted red at the top of the form. An error message a visitor cannot perceive is not an error message; it is a locked door with no handle.

2.6 Readable structure. Pages carry headings in logical order, paragraphs in plain sentences, and a reading order that follows the visual order, so that assistive technology encounters each idea in the sequence a sighted reader would. The Site is built to be read on a small screen as well as a large one, at magnification, and without horizontal scrolling of body text. Chemical precision belongs in the formulation; the prose on this Site aims for the precision of a plain sentence.

2.7 Policy pages are published as live, readable text — never as images. Every policy, statement, and term in this suite, including this one, exists on the Site as text that can be selected, copied, searched, read aloud, translated, and printed. An image of a policy is not a policy a screen reader can read, and it is not a policy a Customer can search at the moment they need one sentence from it.

2.8 Review at every major site change. We review accessibility at every major site change — a new page or section, a redesigned template, a rebuilt form, a new checkout step, or any change to navigation, color, typography, or document structure large enough to alter how the Site is read or moved through. The review is a condition of publication, not a project scheduled afterward. A regression introduced by a change is treated exactly as any other barrier: a defect, found and fixed, and meanwhile answered through Article 4.

Article 3Known Limitations

3.1We are honest about where we are not perfect yet. A limitation stated plainly is a service to the visitor who must rely on the Site; a limitation hidden behind confident language is a second barrier laid over the first. The limitations below are known to us now. If we discover more, they will be named here, in this document, in text, and in the next published version.

3.2 Safety Data Sheet PDFs. Some Safety Data Sheet PDFs follow their manufacturers' formats and may not be fully navigable by screen readers — they may carry untagged text, reading orders that scramble the document, or tables that a screen reader announces as fragments rather than as data. ALETH provides any SDS as accessible text on request, and the request runs through the same channel and the same response standard as every other accessibility request under Article 4. The document is not the point; the information is. If the PDF will not speak, we will.

3.3 Third-party checkout and payment pages. Third-party checkout and payment pages are operated by the payment processor, whose accessibility ALETH does not control. We do not claim their conformance, and we will not pretend that a page we do not operate is a page we can guarantee. What we do control is the choice of partners: we choose partners who share the standard, and we weigh accessibility in that choice. Where such a page does create a barrier, Article 4 applies to it without exception — the Customer is not asked to solve the problem alone because the problem lives one domain over.

3.4 The standing rule. If any part of the Site creates a barrier for you, tell us. We treat it as a defect, because it is one. Not a misunderstanding, not a matter of preference, not a limitation of your tools — a defect, in our work, owned by us. It enters the same order of seriousness as a defect in a formulation, and it is fixed the same way: named, understood, corrected, and verified.

Article 4Contact & Response

4.1 How to reach us. For help, an alternative format, or to report a barrier, write to support@alethchemical.com with "Accessibility" in the subject line. The word in the subject is not a formality: it routes your message to a human who reads it as accessibility work, not as ordinary support traffic to be answered by script. One channel, named and open — no labyrinth of forms, no requirement that a visitor who cannot use the Site fight a second interface to report that they could not use the first.

4.2 The response standard. We respond within 5 Business Days — Business Days meaning Monday through Friday, excluding United States federal holidays — and we provide the information or service you need in a format that works for you. The response is a reply from a person who has understood the barrier, not an acknowledgment that a ticket exists. If the barrier cannot be corrected within those 5 Business Days, the response will say so plainly and tell you what happens next, because an honest timeline is part of the answer.

4.3 What the format commitment means. "A format that works for you" means the format you name: accessible HTML, plain text, large print, a structured document a screen reader can navigate, a plain-language summary, or a reading of the information over the phone or by email — whichever you can actually use. You do not have to justify the request, name a diagnosis, or prove a disability to receive it. We ask what format works. We do not ask why. Requests made on another person's behalf are welcome on the same terms as requests made in person.

4.4 No badges, no overlays. We do not claim third-party "accessibility certification" badges, and we do not deploy accessibility overlay widgets that promise conformance on top of the page. A badge is a claim; a working keyboard path is a fact. We would rather build it right than badge it up.

4.5 If our answer fails you. If the reply does not come, if the format we provide still does not work, or if the barrier remains after it was reported, write again and say exactly that. A second failure is not your burden to absorb in silence; it is a defect in our response, and it escalates on our side with the same seriousness as the first. The door in 4.1 does not close after one use.
